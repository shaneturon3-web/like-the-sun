# Adversarial payload test — four TOM surfaces

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Mode:** read-only thought experiment grounded in source contracts  
**Purpose:** remove each surface’s carrier and observe whether the functional payload survives

## Test protocol

For each TOM surface:

1. identify the declared carrier;
2. remove or flatten that carrier conceptually;
3. state the minimum payload that should survive;
4. record the failure if the result becomes only a summary, explanation, or artifact;
5. preserve the test as a receipt, not as a production instruction.

## Results

### TOM1 — remove order and cargo testing

- Carrier: sequence, alternate order, reader watchdog, cargo load.
- Expected payload: the reader must learn that order changes interpretation and
  that a surviving function is not identical to its carrier.
- Adversarial removal: present the chapters as independent fables in neutral
  order and retain only their morals.
- Failure: the stories remain readable but no longer make the reader carry,
  test, or revise the mechanism. The order warning becomes commentary.
- Result: **payload fails**.

### TOM2 — remove layer separation and loss-bearing selection

- Carrier: camada structure, wrong-lessons layer, bridge status, and explicit
  selection cost.
- Expected payload: later material must pressure earlier material without being
  silently merged into canon.
- Adversarial removal: merge the 16 core stories, 12 wrong lessons, and bridge
  candidate into one clean 29-story sequence; keep provenance as metadata only.
- Failure: attack relationships disappear, “wrong lesson” becomes an ordinary
  chapter label, and the archive story’s capacity-to-act dilemma becomes a
  theme rather than a decision.
- Result: **payload fails**.

### Companion — remove rotation and register switching

- Carrier: domestic/systems registers, artifact matrix, chronological versus
  rotated routes, and reader audit.
- Expected payload: the mechanism must survive a change of register and route,
  not merely be explained by the companion text.
- Adversarial removal: linearize the briefing as an explanatory encyclopedia
  and retain the artifact descriptions without route tests.
- Failure: receipt, gate, vault, hinge, and archive become symbols or examples;
  the reader no longer tests whether sequence and loading order change what is
  true to the observer.
- Result: **payload fails**.

### Tactical — remove charges, scars, and return paths

- Carrier: charge map, missing-branch map, scar ledger, and Companion return
  paths.
- Expected payload: a reduced field manual must make the reader act under load
  while preserving enough absence to feel the removed branch.
- Adversarial removal: cut the long branches and delete the residual object,
  phrase, body, absence, and record scars.
- Failure: Tactical becomes a shorter explanation. The tunnel remains concise
  but no longer points toward a machine room or carries future pressure.
- Result: **payload fails**.

## Adversarial conclusion

All four surfaces fail when their carrier is removed. This is not evidence that
the carriers are identical; it shows that each surface has a load-bearing
interface. The common plugin candidate should therefore expose a transfer
contract, not a universal format:

```yaml
payload: functional capacity that must survive
carrier: surface-specific vehicle
cut: authorized reduction, rotation, or route change
scar: visible evidence of what was removed and where its return lives
test: compare source function with receiving-surface behavior
failure: readable artifact with no operational pressure
```

## Promotion status

This passes the adversarial test for a **plugin candidate grouping**. It does
not yet justify one universal skill: the four carriers require distinct
adapters and distinct failure checks.
